TCM Dietetics

Eat well. Live well. Age well.

In Traditional Chinese Medicine, food is part of treatment. The aim is to eat for your body's current state and the season, not only for calories.

1

WHAT to eat

Foods run warming or cooling. Cold and tired: favor warming foods such as ginger or lamb. Hot and stressed: favor cooling foods such as cucumber or mung beans. Heavy and bloated: favor drying foods such as corn or celery.

2

WHEN to eat

Digestion is strongest around midday. Make lunch (11 am–1 pm) your largest meal and keep dinner (5–7 pm) lighter.

3

HOW to eat

Prefer warm, cooked meals and drink warm water or tea with food. Cold drinks and ice cream weaken digestion.

Five Elements Tea 五行養生茶

Five blends, one for each element and its organ. Educational overview; ask your doctor which suits you.

Wood 木 Liver

Liver-soothing, eye-brightening: chrysanthemum, rose, goji, cassia seed.

Fire 火 Heart

Heart-nourishing, spirit-calming: longan, red dates, lily bulb, ophiopogon (maidong).

Earth 土 Spleen

Balancing for all seasons: poria, astragalus, tangerine peel, goji, red dates.

Metal 金 Lung

Lung-moistening: lily bulb, dried pear, sea coconut, ophiopogon, goji.

Water 水 Kidney

Gently tonifying: dried mulberry, black soybean, black rice, goji.

Body clock: Zi Wu Liu Zhu 子午流注

Zi Wu Liu Zhu holds that the flow of qi and blood follows the rhythm of the day. Aligning meals, rest and treatment with those rhythms is meant to support health.
